## 1. The North Cove failure (IV solutions, Hurricane Helene)

A single facility holding most of the national capacity for one product went
down at once. This is the paper's single-failure scenario, observed.

| Fact | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Plant | Baxter North Cove, Marion NC. Largest Baxter site, ~2,500 workers | AHA special bulletin 2024-09-30; healthexec |
| Share of US IV solutions | ~60% | AHA bulletin; NBC News; FDA/AHA 2025-08-11 |
| Output | 1.5 million bags per day | AHA bulletin; MHA |
| Closed | 29 Sept 2024, flooding | AHA |
| Other manufacturers | B. Braun ~23% (Irvine CA, Daytona Beach FL); ICU Medical (Austin TX) and Fresenius Kabi (Wilson NC, Grand Island NY) the remainder | NBC News; B. Braun PR 2024-10-18; ICU/Otsuka PR 2026-07-09; Fresenius Kabi |

### Allocation timeline (Baxter, based on historical purchases Apr to Sept 2024)

| Date | Allocation | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Oct 2024 | product-code allocations implemented; reported as 40% for direct customers, **10% for distributors** | Healthcare Dive 2024-10-09; AHA bulletin 2024-10-09 |
| 9 Oct 2024 | levels raised and made "consistent across Baxter's direct customers and distribution partners"; basis is each customer's historical ordering 1 March to 31 Aug 2024, by product code. By category: high-demand large-volume saline and dextrose 60%; other large-volume Viaflex 40%; irrigation 40%; small-volume Viaflex 100%; Viaflo 100%; Clinimix 100%; Mini-Bag Plus 100%; sterile water for injection 90%; high-concentration dextrose 75%; designated children's hospitals 100% across all products | Baxter customer letter 2024-10-09 (PDF read, nhia.org copy) |

**Denominator warning.** Baxter's percentages are shares of a customer's own historical Baxter orders, per product code. The model's 40% is remaining national supply over national demand. They are not the same quantity, and the paper treats the North Cove instance as an illustration, not a test.
| 26 Nov 2024 | 0.9% NaCl 1000 mL 60% to 80%; 500 mL and 250 mL 60% to 90% | AHA News 2024-12-02 |
| 7 Nov 2024 | two lines restarted = ~50% of pre-hurricane IV output, ~85% of 1 L output | AHA News 2024-11-07 |
| 28 Jan 2025 | all lines restarted; pre-hurricane levels expected early Q1 2025 | AHA News 2025-01-29 |
| 13 May 2025 | allocations removed on "virtually all" North Cove product codes; several codes were at 100% to 110% just before removal; 1 to 2 week lag through distribution | Baxter customer letter 2025-05-13 (PDF read) |
| 8 Aug 2025 | FDA declares 0.9% NaCl shortage resolved (had been on the list since 2018) | FDA statement 2025-08-08; AHA News 2025-08-11 |

So the allocation floor of 40% appeared immediately after a 60% capacity
loss, which is what proportional allocation predicts. The 10% to distributor
customers at the same moment is the same aggregate output with a different
distribution, decided by channel rather than by any objective.

## 3. Concentration and shortage statistics

### FDA Drug Shortages Task Force, 2019 (PDF read, pp. 5, 109 to 110)
- 163 drugs entered shortage 2013 to 2017 (130 shortages).
- 103 (63%) administered by injection; 109 (67%) had a generic on the market; median age 34.8 years since approval.
- 115 (71%) followed a supply disruption; 101 of those (88%) were tied to quality issues. So 101 of 163, or 62%, were quality-driven.
- "On average, three companies that were approved to market but were not doing so."
- Natural disaster is one of five reason categories; Hurricane Maria 2017 is the example given.

### ASPE / HHS, Analysis of Drug Shortages 2018 to 2023 (NCBI Bookshelf)
- Injectables 50% (n = 1,018) of shortages; oral 42.3%; topical 7.7%.
- Median shortage duration: injectables 4.6 years, oral 1.6 years; essential medicines 4.0 vs 2.3.
- 2018 peak of 466 new shortages; 201 in 2021; 356 in 2023.
- Among documented causes: demand increase 37.3% (n = 499), API shortage 11.1% (n = 148).
- "Forty percent of generic drug markets have a single manufacturer."

### openFDA drug shortages endpoint, pulled 2026-09-10 (`data/fda_shortages.json`)
- 1,619 records: 1,166 Current, 446 To Be Discontinued, 7 Resolved.
- 243 distinct generic names. 175 (72.0%) appear with a single company in the records; 21 with two; 47 with three or more; max 17.
- Among the 70 generics with status Current, 9 (12.9%) have a single company.
- Injection dosage form: 992 of 1,619 records (61.3%). Of 114 injectable generics, 62 (54.4%) list a single company.
- Top five companies hold 636 of 1,619 records (39.3%): Hospira/Pfizer 194, Fresenius Kabi 182, Hikma 98, Teva 88, Pfizer 74.
- `shortage_reason` is blank in 1,196 of 1,619. Where stated: Other 153, demand increase 102, discontinuation 63, API shortage 60, shipping delay 21, GMP compliance 18.

### Pfizer Rocky Mount, tornado July 2023 (CIDRAP, fetched)
- About 25% of Pfizer's sterile injectables; "nearly 8% of all sterile injectables used in US hospitals".
- More than 150 drugs made there; 65 at risk of shortage; fewer than 10 sole-source for the US.
- 3,200 workers; one of 10 US Pfizer plants.

### Other
- Sole-source products were 48% of new shortages in 2026 (AJMC, *search-derived*).
